How this works, and what to trust
  • Everything on this page is real data, no sample numbers: daily page views from the free Wikimedia Pageviews API (human readers only) and split- and dividend-adjusted closing prices from Yahoo Finance. A GitHub Action refreshes the data every weekday evening.
  • Spike: a day whose views are at least the chosen multiple of the median of the previous 60 days. After a spike we ignore further spikes for 10 trading days, so one news story counts once.
  • No peeking: Wikipedia counts days in UTC, which runs past the US close. So we only trade from the close of the first trading day after the spike.
  • What the results do not include: trading costs, taxes, and the fact that these 16 companies were picked because they are famous today (survivorship bias). Results this good or bad on 16 stocks can easily be luck, so treat them as a first look, not proof.
  • The calculations run in your browser from the embedded data; nothing is sent anywhere.
